How Overdraft Works

An overdraft is a credit facility linked to your bank account that lets you withdraw more money than your account balance — up to a pre-approved limit. Think of it as a flexible loan that sits on top of your bank account.

Example
Your account has ₹50,000. Your OD limit is ₹5,00,000. You can spend up to ₹5,50,000 total (your money + borrowed money).

How It Works — Step by Step

  1. Sanction — The bank approves an OD limit based on collateral (FD, property, shares, salary) or your creditworthiness.
  2. Drawing Power — You can withdraw any amount up to the limit, any number of times.
  3. Repayment — Any money deposited back into the account automatically reduces the outstanding OD amount.
  4. Revolving — Unlike a term loan, you can draw and repay repeatedly. The limit stays available as long as the facility is active.
Key Difference from a Loan
With a ₹10L loan, you get ₹10L upfront and pay interest on the full amount from day 1. With a ₹10L OD, you only draw what you need, when you need it — and pay interest only on what you've actually used.

How Interest is Charged

Daily Reducing Balance Method

Interest is calculated daily on the outstanding amount at end of each day, then debited monthly.

Daily Interest = Outstanding Amount × (Annual Rate ÷ 365)
Monthly Interest = Sum of all daily interest charges in that month

Worked Example

DayOutstandingRateDaily Interest
Day 1–10₹5,00,00010%₹5,00,000 × 10% ÷ 365 = ₹136.99/day
Day 11: You deposit ₹3,00,000 → balance drops to ₹2,00,000
Day 11–30₹2,00,00010%₹2,00,000 × 10% ÷ 365 = ₹54.79/day
Monthly interest = (10 × ₹136.99) + (20 × ₹54.79) = ₹1,369.90 + ₹1,095.80 = ₹2,465.70 Compare: Term loan of ₹5L at 10% → ~₹4,109/month regardless of repayment timing.

Key Interest Rules

  • Interest-only payments — Most ODs require you to pay only the interest monthly. Principal repayment is flexible.
  • No prepayment penalty — Since it's revolving, depositing money back is expected, not penalised.
  • Minimum usage — Some banks charge a small commitment fee (0.5–1%) if you don't use the facility at all.
  • Penal interest — If you exceed the limit or miss interest payments, expect 2–4% additional penal interest.
3

Repayment Mechanics

Interest Payment

  • Auto-debited from the same OD account on the 1st of each month (or a specified date).
  • If your OD account doesn't have sufficient credit, the interest gets added to the outstanding — compounding the problem.

Principal Repayment

  • No EMI — This is the biggest advantage. You repay when you have surplus cash.
  • Any deposit = repayment — Salary credits, business receipts, transfers — all automatically reduce your outstanding.
  • Full repayment — Required at renewal (usually annually) or at facility closure.

Renewal

  • Most ODs are sanctioned for 1 year and renewed annually.
  • At renewal, the bank may ask you to bring the balance to zero briefly ("flush out"), reassess your financials, and renew.
  • Property-backed ODs (LAP-OD) can have longer tenures (10–15 years).

Smart Strategies to Minimise OD Interest

  1. Park surplus cash in the OD account — Even parking money for 5 days reduces interest for those 5 days. Route your salary, business receipts, rental income into the OD account first.
  2. Use OD against FD instead of breaking the FD — You keep earning FD interest (say 7%) while paying only 8–9% on the amount you use. Net cost is just 1–2%. Breaking the FD would cost you a penalty + loss of the higher rate.
  3. Sweep-in FD — Some banks offer sweep-in FDs where surplus balance auto-converts to FD and auto-breaks when needed. Good complementary tool.
  4. Flush-out strategy — Before annual renewal, bring the OD balance to zero for a day or two. Banks like this, and it can help you get better renewal terms.

OD on Home Loan — Yes, It Exists

One of the Smartest Financial Products in India
A home loan where the loan account functions like an overdraft. You can park surplus money in it and withdraw anytime — interest is charged only on the net outstanding.

How It Works

Loan sanctioned: ₹50,00,000 at 8.50% for 20 years EMI: ₹43,391/month Month 6: Park ₹5,00,000 surplus in the OD account Interest now on: ₹50L - ₹5L = ₹45,00,000 (not ₹50L) Month 9: Need ₹3L for emergency → withdraw it Interest now on: ₹50L - ₹2L = ₹48,00,000 (remaining parked: ₹2L) Your EMI stays the same, but you save lakhs in interest.

Annual Savings Example

Tax-Free Savings
Unlike FD interest which is taxable, the interest you save by parking in a home loan OD is effectively tax-free. Parking ₹5L gives you 8.5% tax-free effective return.
